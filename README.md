# Two-Fan Regenerative Air-Braking System

A model of a regenerative air-braking system for a car that stores braking energy in a spring and reuses it for forward thrust.

🎥 **Model overview video:** [Watch the overview](description_model_%20overview.mp4)

## Project overview

During braking, a shield drops to expose the front turbine to airflow, generating drag and rotating a drivetrain that drives rack 1 to compress a spring. Once stored, a small spring powered stopper holds the mechanism. During re-acceleration, the stopper is pulled to release the spring and it drives rack 2 through a separate gear system, spinning the rear fan as a propeller to generate forward thrust. The model illustrates the conversion of kinetic energy into elastic potential energy and back into mechanical motion.

## Process

The most challenging part of developing my wind-powered energy-conservation system was converting the turbine’s rotational motion into the linear motion required to compress a spring. After encountering a rack-and-pinion mechanism in a past-paper question, I recognized its potential application to my project and adapted it to address this challenge. I independently performed the engineering calculations using classroom concepts and researched additional formulas when necessary. To redirect the turbine’s rotation by 90 degrees, I consulted my brother, a mechanical engineer, who introduced me to bevel gears, which I incorporated into my design. I used Claude to translate my drawings and descriptions into a 3D CAD model.

For fabrication, I collaborated with a mechanic near my home whose expertise in machining and manufacturing became invaluable. In his small workshop, I learned to operate lathe machines, gear shapers, and milling machines, as well as use welding equipment. Together, we manufactured and assembled custom-sized gears, shafts, stoppers, and other components, including deep-groove ball bearings. This process exposed me to the practical challenges of manufacturing, assembly, and refining mechanical designs beyond theoretical calculations.

## Next steps

Moving forward, I want to investigate lighter, more affordable, and durable materials to improve the system's overall efficiency. I am particularly interested in reducing its weight, minimizing energy losses, and refining its mechanical components to improve energy storage and release.
